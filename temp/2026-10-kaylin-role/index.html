Head of Research and Insight

Leads the research behind every Commercial Foundation: customer interviews, market and competitor research, and the evidence of why buyers choose, hesitate or walk away. What this person finds is what the positioning, the message and the creative are built on.

Why this role exists

Every Assay House client starts with the Commercial Foundation: the work that finds what makes a business genuinely worth choosing, before anything is designed, written or advertised. Most businesses sell something that looks and sounds like their competitors. We don't copy or guess the market. We find the real reason customers choose them, and we hear it from the customers themselves.

The Foundation is only as good as the research underneath it. This role owns that research.

The answers from this role go straight into the client's positioning, their offer, the words on their website and the ideas behind their campaigns. When the research is sharp, everyone after it knows exactly what to say and to whom.

Where the role sits

  • Reports to Tarryn, Head of Marketing and Growth, who turns the findings into positioning and strategy.
  • Works with Client Services, who introduce the client and line up the people to speak to.
  • Hands findings to Copy and Design, who use customers' own words and reasons in the work.
  • Works alongside our AI tools. Much of the desk research, transcription and first drafts are done with AI. This role brings what AI can't: talking to real people, noticing what they really mean, and judging what is true.

What the role involves

Talk to people

Customer interviews

For each Foundation: interviews with the client's customers, people who chose a competitor, people who walked away, and sometimes their staff. In person, by phone or on a video call.

Look around

Market and competitor research

Who the client competes with, what each one promises, what they charge, what their customers praise and complain about in reviews, and where the gap is.

Go out

Field research

Short, friendly conversations with the public where a client's buyers are found: shops, venues, events, car parks. Recorded on a phone, with permission.

Make sense of it

Turn it into insight

Group and count what people said, keep their exact words, and say plainly how sure we are. "Seven of ten said..." rather than "people feel...".

Keep us honest

The evidence behind every claim

Keep a record of where each finding came from, and check that anything a client wants to say publicly ("the fastest", "the first") can actually be backed up.

Share it

Present the findings

Explain what was found to Tarryn, the team and sometimes the client, in plain language, with the customer quotes that make it real.

And the care that comes with it: asking permission properly, keeping people's details and recordings private under POPIA, and never letting research turn into selling.

How research fits into a Foundation

  1. The brief. What the client sells, who buys, and the decision ahead of them. Sets what we need to learn.
  2. Desk research. The market, competitors, reviews and anything already written about the client's buyers.
  3. Interviews. Real conversations with customers and lost prospects about what they actually did and why.
  4. The findings. Why buyers choose, hesitate or walk away, in their own words, with how sure we are.
  5. Into the Foundation Book. The findings shape the positioning, the offer, the message and the 90-day plan.

Examples of the work

Example 1 · "We get leads, but they don't buy"

A client has plenty of enquiries and too few signed deals. You speak to a few prospects who said no, a handful of current clients (including one who left) and some live prospects. Often the answer is something the business never suspected, such as buyers not understanding what they get in the first month. That becomes the new pitch and the follow-up.

Example 2 · A product that looks like everyone else's

A client sells something with many lookalikes. You read through hundreds of competitors' customer reviews and find the complaint that keeps coming up and that nobody solves. Then you check it in interviews. That complaint often becomes the client's strongest reason to be chosen.

Example 3 · Field research for new products

Two new tipping products are in development within the group: one for car guards, petrol attendants and caddies, one for hotel, lodge, restaurant and spa staff. Before either is branded, someone has to go out between George and Plett and hear from workers, owners and the people who tip them. Short conversations, recorded with permission, on a phone page we have already built.

Example 4 · Can we say that?

A client wants their website to say they are "the most trusted" in their area. You find out what proof exists, whether customers actually say it, and suggest wording that is both true and strong.

What good looks like

MeasureWhat we look for
Real voicesFindings built on what people actually did and said, with their exact words, not opinions or guesses.
HonestyLukewarm and negative answers reported alongside the good ones, and a clear "how sure we are".
UsefulSomething the team did not know before, that changes what we say or who we say it to.
On timeResearch finished in time for the Foundation, with notes written up within a day of each conversation.
CarePermission asked properly, details kept private, nobody pressured.

Who does well in this role

  • Genuinely curious about people, and comfortable starting a conversation with a stranger.
  • Puts anyone at ease, from a car guard to a company owner, and can switch language when it helps.
  • Listens more than they talk, and notices the thing someone says almost in passing.
  • Organised: keeps track of who was spoken to, what they agreed to, and what is still outstanding.
  • Writes and speaks plainly, without dressing findings up.
  • Comfortable using a phone, AI tools and simple online forms as part of the work.
