Head of Research and Insight

Before we design or write anything for a client, we find out why their customers actually buy. This role is the person who goes and asks: surveys, phone calls and interviews, and trying out the sales scripts we write for clients.

Works withTarryn, who prepares every task and reviews the results

Why it matters

Every client starts with what we call the Commercial Foundation: finding the real reason their customers choose them, and the words those customers use. We don't guess. We go and ask real people.

You are the person who goes and asks. Everything we build for a client starts with what you bring back.

What the work is

Face to face

Quick surveys

A few short questions, asked in person, often by walking up to people in a mall, a car park or another busy public place. You tick the answers and jot down anything extra.

Phone and in person

Customer interviews

Longer conversations with a client's customers, in person at a business we have arranged beforehand, or over the phone. Recorded with permission, so we keep people's exact words.

Phone

Trying out sales scripts

When we write a sales script for a client, you phone the kind of businesses or people the client wants to win, using the script, and ask about their problems and what they look for. Recorded with permission, so we can sharpen the script before we hand it to the client.

After each one

Write-ups

What people said, written up clearly and honestly, in their own words where it matters, so the team can work from it.

What it is not: you don't manage clients or speak for Assay House to our clients. Tarryn handles client relationships. Your calls are to the people our clients want to reach, using scripts we prepare with you.

How each job works

  1. You never go in cold. For every task you get the questions or the script, who you are speaking to and where, and we prepare you for that job before you start.
  2. You do the calls, surveys or interviews in the agreed window, at times that suit you.
  3. Permission first, every time. Every recording starts by asking the person's permission. If they say no, nothing is recorded and you take notes instead.
  4. You write it up within a day, and note how long each conversation took and anyone who said no thanks.
  5. We go through it together, so the next round is better.

Behind the scenes. Now and then we may ask you to film yourself doing an interview, for our behind-the-scenes videos. We agree that with you first, and anyone else on camera is asked for their permission too.

Examples of the work

Example 1 · A morning of quick surveys

We are researching how people tip car guards and petrol attendants. You spend a morning at a shopping centre and a petrol station in George, asking drivers five quick questions each and ticking their answers. About five minutes a person.

Example 2 · Customer interviews

A client wants to know why some customers stay and others leave. We arrange the introductions, and you speak to six of their customers, two in person and four by phone, about the last time they bought and what nearly stopped them.

Example 3 · Testing a sales script

We have written a sales script for a client who sells to restaurants. You phone a list of restaurants that fit their ideal customer, follow the script, and ask what problems they have with their current supplier and what they look for. We listen back together, see where people lost interest or leaned in, and improve the script before the client uses it.

Who it suits

  • Friendly and confident, enjoys talking to strangers, and is not thrown by the occasional rude one.
  • Listens more than they talk, and is curious about why people choose what they choose.
  • Comfortable on the phone, including calling people who weren't expecting it.
  • Can write up a conversation clearly and honestly.
  • Organised and reliable with times, lists and follow-ups.

No marketing background needed. We train you on how we run surveys, interviews and calls.

Time, pay and place

About 30 to 40 hours a month, flexible on days and times: for example two mornings or afternoons a week, one full day a week, or two full days every second week. Busier months may need a little more, quieter ones less.

Pay is discussed in the interview, and invoiced monthly. This is temp and contract work, not a permanent position. Own transport is needed for visits.
